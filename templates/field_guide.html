Learning to find peelers.

This page will hold molt-phase sketches, visual cues, and field notes for training new green crab harvest workers.

Visual cues to check first

These sketches are training aids for human review. Look for several cues together, then compare your read with MoltMeter and known molt timing examples.

Side split

Check the side edge of the carapace for a visible seam or opening. A more obvious split can indicate the crab is close to molt.

Halo at joints

Look around leg joints and limb bases for pale rings or halos. The cue is easiest to see in good light and close views.

Shell and limb opacity

As molt approaches, shell and limbs may look duller, milkier, or less translucent. Compare against crabs from the same light and tank.

Before molt, the old cuticle separates from the epidermis, then the shell opens along a weak line when the crab emerges. After molt, the new shell is pale and soft before it hardens. In practice, the strongest reads usually come from several cues together, not just one.
